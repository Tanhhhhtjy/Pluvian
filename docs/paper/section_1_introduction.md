# §1 Introduction (draft 2026-06-14)

Short-term precipitation nowcasting over North China presents a distinctive operational challenge. Warm-season convection in the region is short-lived, often organising and dissipating within an hour, so forecasts at sub-10 minute output cadence and sub-two-hour lead are directly tied to disaster response timelines for urban flooding, transport, and outdoor safety. Operational nowcasting systems still rely predominantly on radar extrapolation and optical-flow advection [Germann 2002; Pulkkinen 2019], which capture short-term motion but lack a physical representation of convective initiation and decay. Deep-learning nowcasters have closed much of this gap on bulk skill scores [Ravuri 2021; Gao 2022; Zhang 2023; Andrychowicz 2023], yet skill at the high-intensity tail above 30 mm h⁻¹ remains a documented weakness across architectures and training regimes.

A second, less-discussed gap concerns the input side. Radar reflectivity alone provides limited information about the lower-tropospheric moisture reservoir and the synoptic environment in which convection grows. Ground-based GNSS retrievals of precipitable water vapour (PWV) deliver column-integrated moisture at minute cadence, and ERA5 reanalysis [Hersbach 2020] supplies low-level wind, humidity, and instability fields at hourly resolution. Combining these heterogeneous sources is well-motivated physically but remains technically difficult: the modalities differ in native resolution, sampling geometry, and update frequency, and naive concatenation often degrades rather than improves skill. Existing multimodal nowcasters typically report a single fused configuration, leaving the marginal contribution of each modality opaque.

A third gap concerns how physical knowledge is injected into training. Physically motivated PDE-residual losses — continuity, water budget, or mass conservation — are increasingly used as auxiliary terms to enforce "physical consistency" between predicted fields [Beucler 2021; de Bezenac 2018]. Aggregate metrics such as CSI@1 or FSS typically improve under these losses, but the impact on extreme-intensity skill is rarely diagnosed separately. Because convective extremes occupy a small fraction of pixels, bulk-averaged scores can mask substantial degradation of the tail. The community lacks a diagnostic that distinguishes whether a model preserves intense cores in a slightly displaced location (a tolerable failure for nowcasting) or smooths them away entirely (a physically misleading failure).

This work addresses the three gaps through a single experimental pipeline on 6-minute, 108-minute-horizon nowcasts over North China, and contributes the following.

**(i) A multimodal nowcasting baseline with monotonic fusion ablations.** We construct three nested configurations — radar-only (ab1), +PWV (ab2), +ERA5 (ab3) — sharing identical backbone, schedule, and loss. The cascade yields monotonic improvement across CSI@{1,5,10}, FSS@{3,11}, and MAE on both an event-centred and a robustness test split; MAE drops from 5.19 to 4.27 mm h⁻¹ (-17.7%) across the full cascade. The ablation also surfaces a non-monotone behaviour at CSI@30 that foreshadows a sharper tension reported in (iii).

**(ii) Pooled-CSI as a smoothing-vs-displacement diagnostic.** We define a single-parameter extension of CSI in which both forecast and observation are max-pooled over a neighbourhood of radius w before thresholding. A spatially displaced but intense forecast recovers skill as w grows; a physically smoothed forecast does not. The diagnostic is architecture- and loss-agnostic and can be reported alongside standard scores at negligible cost.

**(iii) An architectural — not loss-level — prescription for extreme preservation.** Adding a water-budget residual loss on top of ab3 improves bulk skill further but collapses CSI@30 from 0.291 to 0.210 on event_test (-28%) and from 0.276 to 0.215 on test_robust (-22%), with 0 of 18 forecast leads positive. Pooled-CSI confirms the failure as smoothing rather than displacement. We then show that a Cubic Dual Upsampling (CDU) decoder, combining bicubic low-frequency interpolation with PixelShuffle high-frequency residuals, restores extreme skill without reweighting losses.

Section §2 describes the data, fusion architecture, and pooled-CSI definition. Section §3 reports the three results acts: stepwise fusion, the budget-loss extreme collapse, and the CDU recovery. Section §4 discusses implications for physics-constrained nowcasting and the limits of the present study.
